# How Social Media Algorithms Influence People

**Final University Presentation**
**English level:** A2 – B1 (closer to A2)
**Length:** 10 slides, 7–10 minute speech
**Topic area:** data analytics, social media, algorithms, user behavior

---

## Global Design Guide

**Style:** modern, clean, minimal, "data-tech" look.

**Color palette (use 3–4 colors only):**
- Primary: `#1E1E2E` (deep dark blue – background or accents)
- Secondary: `#5A8DEE` (soft blue – titles, lines)
- Accent: `#FF6B6B` (warm red – important numbers, warnings)
- Background: `#F7F8FA` (almost white) and `#FFFFFF`
- Text: `#222831` on light, `#FFFFFF` on dark

**Fonts (free, modern, easy to read):**
- Headings: **Montserrat Bold** or **Poppins SemiBold**, size 32–40 pt
- Body text: **Inter Regular** or **Open Sans**, size 20–24 pt
- Numbers / statistics: **Poppins Bold**, size 48–60 pt

**Visual elements:**
- Flat icons (Flaticon, Lucide, or Iconify)
- Soft shadows, rounded corners (12–16 px)
- Plenty of white space
- 1 big image OR 1 big number per slide — not both
- Use brand logos (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook) as simple flat icons

**Animations (keep them subtle):**
- Slide content: **Fade In** (0.3 s)
- Numbers / statistics: **Zoom In** (0.4 s)
- Bullet lists: **Appear one by one** on click
- Avoid spinning, bouncing, or 3D effects — they look unprofessional

# POSSIBLE TEACHER QUESTIONS (with simple answers)

**Q1. What is an algorithm in one sentence?**
A: An algorithm is a set of rules that a computer follows to make a decision.

**Q2. Why do TikTok and Instagram know me so well?**
A: Because they collect a lot of data — every like, every second I watch a video — and they use it to predict what I will enjoy next.

**Q3. What is a "filter bubble"?**
A: It is a situation when the algorithm shows me only one point of view, so I do not see other opinions.

**Q4. Are algorithms always bad?**
A: No. They also help us learn new things, find friends, and discover new hobbies. The problem is how we use them.

**Q5. Can social media really cause depression?**
A: Some studies say yes, especially for teenagers who use it more than three hours a day. But it depends on the person and the type of content.

**Q6. What can we do to use social media in a safe way?**
A: We can set time limits, turn off notifications, follow positive accounts, and take regular breaks from the phone.

**Q7. Why does fake news spread so fast?**
A: Because it is shocking and emotional, and the algorithm shows emotional posts to more people to get more clicks.

**Q8. Do you personally use social media a lot?**
A: Yes, like most students. But after this project, I started to use the screen time function and I follow fewer accounts now.

**Q9. Which app has the strongest algorithm, in your opinion?**
A: TikTok, because it learns my taste very fast — sometimes in less than ten minutes.

**Q10. Where did you find your information?**
A: I used reports from MIT, JAMA, Gallup, and articles from BBC and The Guardian about the Cambridge Analytica case.

# TIPS FOR CONFIDENT DELIVERY

**Before the presentation:**
- Practice your speech **out loud at least 3 times**, not only in your head.
- Record yourself on the phone and listen — check pronunciation and speed.
- Time yourself: aim for **8 minutes** so you have a small buffer.
- Prepare **paper cards** with key words (not the whole text).

**During the presentation:**
- Speak **slower than you think** — A2/B1 speakers often rush when nervous.
- Look at **3 points in the room** (left, center, right), not at the floor.
- Pause for **1 second** after each important sentence — it sounds professional.
- If you forget a word, say *"in other words…"* and use a simpler word. Do not stop.
- Smile at the start and at the end. It helps you relax.

**Body language:**
- Stand straight, shoulders open, hands visible.
- Use one hand to point to the screen — do not turn your back to the audience.
- Don't hold the phone or play with a pen.

**For the Q&A:**
- If you don't understand a question, ask politely: *"Could you repeat the question, please?"*
- If you don't know the answer, say: *"That is a very good question. I did not study this part, but I think…"*
- Always finish your answer with a short sentence — do not leave it open.

**Mental tricks:**
- Before you start, take **3 slow breaths**.
- Remember: the teacher wants you to succeed.
- Even native speakers make mistakes — small mistakes are normal and OK.
