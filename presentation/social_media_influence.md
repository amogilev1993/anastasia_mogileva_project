## Global Design Guide

**Style:** modern, clean, minimal, "social-tech" look.

**Color palette (use 3–4 colors only):**
- Primary: `#1E1E2E` (deep dark blue – titles, accents)
- Secondary: `#7C5CFF` (soft violet – titles, highlights)
- Accent positive: `#7BC47F` (soft green – good effects)
- Accent warning: `#FF6B6B` (warm red – warnings, key numbers)
- Background: `#F7F8FA` / `#FFFFFF`
- Text: `#222831` on light, `#FFFFFF` on dark
- Muted: `#6B7280`

**Fonts (free, modern, easy to read):**
- Headings: **Montserrat Bold** or **Poppins SemiBold**, 32–40 pt
- Body: **Inter Regular** or **Open Sans**, 20–24 pt
- Big numbers / stats: **Poppins Bold**, 60–90 pt

**Visual rules:**
- Flat icons (Flaticon, Lucide, Iconify)
- Rounded corners 12–16 px, soft shadows, lots of white space
- One big visual OR one big number per slide
- Logos of TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook as flat icons

**Animations (subtle):**
- Slide content — **Fade In** 0.3 s
- Bullets — **Appear one by one** on click
- Numbers — **Zoom In** 0.4 s
- Avoid spin / bounce / 3D effects

**Canva / PowerPoint tips:**
- In **PowerPoint**: use the *Berlin* or *Frame* template, then change colors to the palette above.
- In **Canva**: search for the template **"Minimalist Pitch Deck"** or **"Education Presentation – Modern"**; replace colors and fonts.
- Set slide size to **16:9** (Widescreen).
- Use **Pexels** or **Unsplash** for free photos (search: "smartphone", "teen social media", "friends online").

# POSSIBLE TEACHER QUESTIONS (with simple answers)

**Q1. What is social media in one sentence?**
A: Social media are websites and apps that help people share photos, videos, and ideas online.

**Q2. Which platform is the most popular today?**
A: TikTok and Instagram are the most popular among young people; Facebook is still very big for older users.

**Q3. Is social media good or bad?**
A: Both. It depends on how we use it. With balance, it is useful; without limits, it can harm our time and mental health.

**Q4. How can social media help education?**
A: Through free YouTube tutorials, online study groups, and easy practice of foreign languages with native speakers.

**Q5. What is cyberbullying?**
A: It is when people are aggressive or cruel to others in online comments and messages.

**Q6. What is FOMO?**
A: FOMO means "fear of missing out" — the feeling that everyone has a better life than you because of social media posts.

**Q7. How much time should we spend on social media?**
A: There is no perfect number, but most experts say less than 2 hours a day is healthy.

**Q8. Do you personally feel any negative effects?**
A: Yes, sometimes I scroll for too long and I feel tired. Now I set a daily time limit on my phone.

**Q9. How can we stop being addicted?**
A: Small steps — turn off notifications, set time limits, and don't use the phone before sleep.

**Q10. Where did you find your information?**
A: I used Pew Research (2023), CDC (2021), Statista (2024), and articles from BBC and The Guardian.

# TIPS FOR CONFIDENT DELIVERY

**Before:**
- Practice **out loud at least 3 times** — not just in your head.
- Record yourself on the phone and listen to your pronunciation.
- Time yourself: aim for **8 minutes** to leave a small buffer.
- Prepare **paper cards** with key words (not the full text).

**During:**
- Speak **slower than you think**. A2/B1 speakers often rush.
- Look at **3 points** in the room: left, center, right.
- Pause **1 second** after important sentences — it sounds professional.
- If you forget a word, say *"in other words…"* and use a simpler one.
- Smile at the start and at the end — it relaxes both you and the audience.

**Body language:**
- Stand straight, shoulders open, hands visible.
- Use one hand to point at the screen — never turn your back.
- Don't hold the phone or play with a pen.

**For Q&A:**
- If you don't understand: *"Could you repeat the question, please?"*
- If you don't know: *"That is a good question. I did not study this part, but I think…"*
- Always close your answer with a short sentence.

**Mental tricks:**
- Take **3 slow breaths** before you start.
- Remember: the teacher wants you to succeed.
- Small mistakes are normal — even native speakers make them.
